Promotional merchandise is most effective when the product has a genuine role in the recipient's day. Lanyards are a strong example because they combine practical identification with an unusually visible branding area.

At exhibitions, conferences, workplaces, universities, schools and healthcare environments, lanyards can become part of how people identify themselves and navigate a shared space. That makes them useful not only as accessories but also as tools for creating a more organised and recognisable environment.

01

Why Lanyards Have Real Branding Potential

Unlike many promotional products that sit on a desk or disappear into a bag, lanyards are normally worn where they can be seen. This creates a natural opportunity for a company name, logo, campaign message or visual identity to remain visible while the wearer moves around.

Their usefulness also gives them a practical purpose beyond advertising. A lanyard can hold an identification card, visitor pass, access badge or event credential. Because the product is connected to something useful, recipients have a clear reason to keep wearing it.

The best branded merchandise does not interrupt a person's routine — it becomes part of the routine.

02

Healthcare & Organisations

Combine function with recognition

04

Using Colour as an Organisational Tool

Colour can do more than make a lanyard visually attractive. In the right environment, different colours can help people understand roles, groups or access levels at a glance.

For a large event, for example, organisers could use one colour for staff, another for speakers and another for exhibitors. The exact system will depend on the event's requirements, but the principle is simple: visual differences can make information easier to process.

This is particularly useful when combined with consistent branding. The lanyard can carry the organisation's visual identity while colour provides an additional layer of practical information.

Event Team

A distinct colour can help attendees quickly recognise people responsible for event support.

Speakers & Presenters

A separate visual treatment can make important event roles easier to identify.

Exhibitors

A consistent colour can help distinguish participating businesses or partner groups.

Special Access

Where appropriate, another colour can identify specific access or operational roles.

Explore a practical approach to event identification

  • Keep colour choices easy to distinguish from one another.
  • Use the same colour system consistently throughout the event.
  • Make the main organisation or event branding remain clearly visible.
  • Explain the colour system to staff before the event begins.
  • Consider accessibility when selecting contrasting colours.
05

Designing a Lanyard People Are Happy to Wear

Good lanyard design needs to balance branding with comfort and visual clarity. A design can be bold without becoming cluttered. The organisation's logo should remain recognisable, while supporting text should be kept simple enough to work at the scale of the product.

Repetition can be useful because the lanyard wraps around the wearer and may be viewed from different directions. Repeating a logo or visual motif can create consistent recognition rather than relying on one isolated branding area.

Colour selection should also be intentional. Strong contrast can improve readability, while colours connected to the wider brand identity can make the lanyard feel like a genuine part of the organisation rather than a generic promotional accessory.

06

Branded Lanyards as Part of an Event System

A successful event usually involves many small details working together. Name badges, signage, exhibition stands, staff clothing and promotional products all contribute to the environment attendees experience.

Lanyards can connect several of these elements. Matching the lanyard with event colours, badge layouts and other printed materials creates visual continuity. That consistency can make an event feel more professional and easier to navigate.

The lanyard can also become a subtle reminder of the event after it has finished. If attendees continue using it at work or other events, the branding can remain visible beyond the original occasion.

07

How to Plan a Better Lanyard Order

Good results usually come from making the important decisions before artwork is finalised. The process does not need to be complicated, but it should consider both the practical requirements and the intended brand impression.

Define the users

Identify whether the lanyards are intended for delegates, employees, students, visitors, exhibitors, organisers or several groups.

Decide the function

Determine whether the lanyard will primarily carry an ID card, event pass, access credential or another attachment.

Select the format

Compare material, width, finish and additional features according to the setting and intended use.

Build the artwork

Keep logos, colours and text clear while ensuring the design works across the available branding area.

Plan distribution

Make sure the quantity and role-based allocation match the number of people who will actually need the lanyards.

08

Where Branded Lanyards Can Add Value

Lanyards are particularly versatile because they can serve both promotional and operational purposes. They can be used during trade shows, exhibitions, freshers' week, staff rollouts and professional workplace environments.

Their usefulness can also make them suitable for organisations that want branding to feel integrated rather than overly promotional. Instead of handing someone an item with no clear purpose, a business can provide something directly connected to participation, identification or access.

This combination of utility and visibility is what makes lanyards particularly interesting within a wider promotional merchandise strategy.

09

Making the Brand Feel Consistent

A lanyard should not exist in isolation from the rest of the brand. When possible, its colours and visual treatment should connect with the organisation's existing identity.

Consider how the lanyard looks beside a staff uniform, event signage, printed badges, exhibition graphics or digital materials. Consistency across these touchpoints helps create a stronger overall impression because people encounter familiar visual cues in several places.

The result is not necessarily a louder brand. In many cases, it is a more coherent one — a brand that feels organised because its smallest details support the same visual language.

Making the Lanyard Work Harder for the Brand

A branded lanyard may be a relatively small item, but its role can extend well beyond holding an ID badge. It can help people identify teams, create visual consistency, support event organisation and keep a company's identity visible throughout the day.

The key is to start with purpose. Choose the format according to the setting, design the branding for clarity, use colour intelligently and make sure the final product feels appropriate for the people wearing it. With that approach, a lanyard becomes a useful part of the brand experience rather than simply another promotional giveaway.
